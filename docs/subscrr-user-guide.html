01 · The product

What Subscrr does

Banks show you money after it leaves. Subscrr shows you before — that one-day-early warning is the entire value proposition.

1

Track everything recurring

Every subscription in one ledger: price, currency, billing cycle, category, payment method, renewal date, notes. Add manually in seconds, paste a confirmation email for parsing, or drop a receipt image into the AI Scanner.

2

See the honest numbers

Yearly plans are normalised to true monthly equivalents, mixed currencies convert to one display currency using live exchange rates, and the real 12-month commitment is always visible — not hidden in twelve separate charges.

3

Never be surprised

A background engine runs daily and generates renewal alerts (3 days and 1 day before), free-trial expiry warnings, and budget-threshold alerts — delivered as in-app notifications and real emails.

4

Analyse & optimise

Category breakdowns, a 12-month spend projection, top-expense ranking, payment-method mix, and concrete cost-optimization suggestions quantify exactly which subscriptions to cancel or switch to annual billing.

The public landing page — positioning the product around the "subscription leak" problem, with the interactive calculator one scroll away.
The problem in numbers: industry studies consistently put forgotten-subscription waste at $200+ per person per year — mostly from trials that converted silently and annual renewals nobody remembered. Subscrr's alert engine exists to make that category of loss impossible.

02 · Onboarding

Getting started — five minutes to first value

1
Create your account

/register — email, password (8+ characters), display name. Passwords are bcrypt-hashed; login is rate-limited and protected by per-account lockout. Forgot your password later? The reset flow emails a real, single-use link valid for one hour.

2
Set your currency and budget

/dashboard/settings — pick your display currency (USD, EUR, GBP, INR, JPY, CAD, AUD) and set a monthly budget target. Everything on the dashboard is measured against this number.

3
Add your subscriptions — three ways

Manual entry (name, amount, cycle, renewal date, category, colour) · paste billing text from any confirmation email into the Scanner's text tab · or drop a receipt/invoice image into the AI Scanner and let the vision model fill the form.

4
Read your dashboard

Monthly spend, annual commitment, daily burn, next charge — recalculated live. Yearly plans appear as their true monthly equivalent, so nothing hides in the annual layer.

5
Let the alerts work

From then on the daily engine watches every renewal date, trial end and your budget line. You act on a notification — or deliberately ignore one. Cancelling becomes a decision, never a discovery.

Clean sign-in — inline validation, lockout messaging, no clutter.

What you never have to do

Connect a bank account. Install anything. Trust a third-party aggregator with read access to your finances. Subscrr works on what you tell it — and extracts it for you from the documents you already have.

04 · Feature deep-dive

Managing subscriptions — the full workflow

The subscription ledger: card grid or compact table, live search, category pills, status tabs, one-click actions.

Find anything fast

Real-time search across names and notes; filter by category (AI Tools, Entertainment, Music, Development, Design, Cloud, Productivity, Health, Gaming, Utilities); tab by status (Active / Trial / Paused / Cancelled); sort by renewal date, amount, name or date added.

Pause, don't lose

Pausing freezes a subscription's impact on every total without deleting its history — perfect for seasonal services. Resuming restores it instantly. Cancellation keeps the record for your annual review.

Own your data

One-click export of the full ledger to CSV (spreadsheet-ready, formula-injection-sanitised) or JSON (backup / import-ready). No lock-in: your financial data leaves whenever you ask it to.

The editing model: every field is editable after creation — when Netflix raises prices or you switch a plan from monthly to annual, edit the subscription and every metric, projection, calendar entry and alert recomputes immediately. Amounts are stored as integer cents, so there is no rounding drift anywhere in the system.

05 · Feature deep-dive

AI Receipt Scanner — real extraction, zero fabrication

The scanner: drop a real receipt or invoice image, watch the scan animation, get an editable result card with a confidence meter.

What the AI reads

Merchant/service name · the actually printed amount · currency symbol or code · billing cycle (daily → yearly) · likely category and brand colour · next renewal date · and its own confidence score, shown to you honestly.

What it never does

Invent data. Non-subscription documents produce a clear error. Amounts it cannot determine come back as zero with a "confirm this" note — never a plausible-looking guess. Demo/sample receipts do not exist in the product.

Two input modes

Image tab: drag-and-drop or browse — a scan-line animation plays while the vision model reads the document. Text tab: paste any confirmation email or bank statement line — parsed locally, clearly labelled "Basic local parse" in the engine badge.

One click to ledger

The result card is fully editable before saving. Confirm, and the subscription lands in the ledger — dashboard, analytics, calendar and alerts all update immediately. The average add takes under ten seconds.

The renewal calendar

Every charge mapped to its exact day of month, with month navigation, a "Today" jump, per-day totals and a day inspector.

Read the month like a bill

The notification centre: unread badge, alert cards with context, animated read-check, and permanent dismissal.

Settings, currencies & data portability

Settings: profile, display currency, monthly budget, per-alert email preferences and data export.

Seven currencies, live rates

USD, EUR, GBP, INR, JPY, CAD, AUD. Amounts are stored per-subscription in their original currency and integer cents; conversion to your display currency uses live exchange rates, with resilient fallback rates if the rate service is unreachable.

Budget that means something

The monthly budget target feeds the dashboard gauge and the budget-alert engine — set it once, and every month the system watches the line for you.

Email preferences

Per-alert-type toggles for the email channel: renewals, trials, budget, product. Unsubscribe from email noise without losing in-app alerts.

Data portability: full CSV and JSON export of your ledger is one click away in Subscriptions and Settings. Your financial record is yours — no support ticket, no export fee, no lock-in. Combined with self-hostable SQLite storage, "what happens if I leave?" has a boring, correct answer: you take the file.

13 · Trust

Security & privacy — every protection, explained

LayerWhat is actually enforced
CredentialsPasswords hashed with bcrypt — never stored or logged in plain text. JWTs are signed with a pinned HS256 algorithm and a ≥32-character secret; production refuses to boot without one.
Brute-force defenceTwo independent layers: IP rate limiting (login attempts budgeted per window) and per-account lockout — five failed passwords lock the account for a cool-off period, protecting one targeted victim even from distributed attempts.
Password resetTokens are stored hashed (a database leak reveals nothing usable), single-use, and expire after one hour. The reset page never discloses whether an email exists.
Session validationEvery protected request carries a Bearer token verified server-side; stale sessions are cleared silently. Page-load revalidation runs on a separate rate budget so normal usage never locks you out.
Private filesUploaded receipts are served only with a valid token — anonymous enumeration of the uploads directory is impossible.
Input & output safetyEvery write validated by strict schemas; every SQL query parameterised (no injection surface); CSV exports sanitised against formula injection when opened in Excel.
Transport & headersHSTS, strict Content-Security-Policy on API surfaces, no server fingerprinting, explicit CORS allowlist — never a wildcard in production.
Money integrityAmounts stored as integer cents end-to-end — no floating-point drift can ever distort a total.
Data ownershipSelf-hostable SQLite files, full export anytime, and no bank-credential aggregation anywhere in the product. The attack surface of "nothing to connect" is zero.

15 · Under the hood

Technical architecture & deployment

Stack

Next.js 16 (React 19, Tailwind 4) frontend · Express 4 + TypeScript API with Zod validation · SQLite with versioned migrations and integer-cent money · Payload 3 CMS with generated migrations · Nodemailer/Resend email · OpenAI-compatible vision · Docker + GitHub Actions CI.

One service, one port

A unified server (server.mjs) mounts the compiled Express API and the compiled Next.js app — including Payload's admin, REST and GraphQL — on a single HTTP listener. One deploy, one URL, one health check (/api/health verifies the database live).

Two data stores

App data (users, subscriptions, notifications, activity, leads) and content data (posts, media, authors) live in separate SQLite databases — a data breach or corruption in one never touches the other. Both survive redeploys on a persistent volume.

Operations

Daily alert cron at 00:05 · idempotent migrations and seeds on every boot · rate limiting scoped per surface · structured activity logging · automated CI (typecheck, 35-test integration suite, builds, Docker images) on every push.

Deploying: one Render web service from the included Blueprint (render.yaml) — build command npm run build:render, start command npm run start:all, health check /api/health, persistent disk at /opt/data for both databases, uploads and media. Full walkthrough: docs/DEPLOY-RENDER.md; complete environment-variable reference: the repository README.

16 · Answers

FAQ & troubleshooting

Do I have to connect my bank?

No — and the product never asks. Subscrr works on entries you create or documents you scan. That is a deliberate privacy stance, not a missing feature.

What happens if the AI misreads a receipt?

The result card is fully editable before saving, and a confidence meter flags low-certainty reads. The scanner never invents values: unknown amounts come back as zero with a note, non-subscription documents are rejected with a clear error.

Does pausing delete history?

No. Paused subscriptions are excluded from all totals but kept in the ledger; resuming restores everything instantly.

How do alerts reach me?

In-app notifications always; email whenever a provider is configured (Resend or any SMTP — Gmail app passwords work). Each alert type can be toggled per channel in Settings.

I didn't get a reset email.

Check spam first. If the deployment has no email provider configured, the platform logs reset links instead of sending them — the operator needs to add SMTP_* or RESEND_API_KEY environment variables. /api/health reports the active email transport.

"Too many requests" on login?

The brute-force limiter did its job. Wait for the window to reset or follow the on-screen cool-off message. Page-load session checks are budgeted separately and never cause this.

Can I use it in two currencies?

Yes — that's the point. Each subscription keeps its original currency; your dashboard converts everything to your display currency with live rates.

Where does my data live, and how do I leave?

In SQLite files on the deployment's persistent disk (self-hostable by design). Export the full ledger to CSV/JSON anytime from Subscriptions or Settings — the file is complete and standard.

Who can see my subscriptions?

Only you. Even platform admins see counts, not contents — the admin portal deliberately exposes no per-user subscription data.

What's on the roadmap?

Bank auto-sync (opt-in aggregation for those who want it), per-file signed URLs for receipts, and multi-instance scaling via Postgres + object storage — the current single-file design is the deliberate v1 trade-off for privacy and simplicity.
